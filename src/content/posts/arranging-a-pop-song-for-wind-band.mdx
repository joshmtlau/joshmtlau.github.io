During my days in [NTU Wind Band](https://www.instagram.com/ntuwindband/), I
arranged a few pieces for community concerts and, or occasionally for the band's
encore pieces. I wanted to share one arrangement that went a bit viral on the 
internet. Be warned that it's really sloppy! We didn't have much time to rehearse it.

## The Arrangement
If you hang around on the internet long enough, 
you've probably heard this 80s pop classic ;). 
I'll just highlight some interesting parts of the arrangement:

### Original Solos
The song structure of Never Gonna Give You Up is really simple. Aside from the
bridge, virtually it's just verse and chorus repeated.
While this is probably why the song is so catchy, my experience was that 
transcribing as-is would make it boring for the players.
That's why I added an alto saxophone solo and a trumpet solo to the arrangement.
They're pretty diabolical for soloists though, and we ended up having to cut the 
trumpet solo because our soloist didn't have time to pick it up.

### Fade outs! 
Classic feature of the songs of the 80s; quite hard to replicate for wind band.
Here, I was lucky to stumble upon an orchestral arrangement of EDM
that handles this effect fantastically:
[Aquamarine - Jeremy Blake, arranged by David Bruce](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uuSvosav0sU).
I mimicked their fade out techniques, removing instruments with rich harmonics 
one by one, before ending the piece with a rain stick.
That said, in this performance, we used a windchime instead because that was 
what we were already using in other pieces.



You can find the full arrangement on this [Musescore link](https://musescore.com/user/73520/scores/6874611?share=copy_link).

## The Performance
This recording was from the first concert we had after COVID-19. 
It was really hard for us to rehearse for the concert, because people caught COVID left and right. 

We performed this piece at a community concert (sadly, people there didn't know 
what rickrolling is). That's why we decided to play it again for the encore of 
this concert, where we would have an audience that could appreciate the joke. 
We didn't have much time to rehearse though, because we only decided this one 
day before the concert. I guess the sloppiness actually added to the joke?

### Uploading of the Video
On April fool's day, 2025, we uploaded the video to YouTube.
As with the tradition of rickrolling, we originally uploaded the video under the disguise of 
熱愛105度的你 (Love you at 105°C), another meme song in the Chinese-speaking internet. 
We switched the title and thumbnail back to normal after a few weeks.

### Gaining Views
可拉西可的古典樂總會 Kelashike, A Taiwanese page doing classical music memes, 
shared the video on their [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1FW2KM5JM7/).
台灣達人秀, a Taiwanese entertainment media, also asked to feature the video on 
[their page](https://www.facebook.com/wwwttshow/videos/1467712297936376). 
It did not go crazily viral, but it quickly became the most viewed video on the 
band's YouTube channel. As of November 2025, the video has accumulated around 
50,000 views.
